## Proposed title
**From AI Answers to Accountable Decisions: Building a Human-Led Game Operating Copilot**

## Summary / nominee profile
Joyce Mi is Founder of GGC Games, a global game-operations and AI-product venture. Across more than 15 years at Microsoft, Tencent, Zynga and Gameloft, followed by entrepreneurship, she has worked across game-product strategy, analytics, LiveOps, monetisation, international publishing and operating teams. She authored *Game Operations and Global Expansion: Strategies, Methods, and Skills* (China Machine Press, 2024, byline Ai Xiaomi). Her current work explores how to make AI-supported operating decisions traceable, testable and accountable to a named human leader.

## Proposed nomination answer 1 — Innovation and the problem
The challenge Joyce addresses is a gap between abundant product data and accountable commercial decisions. Game and consumer-app teams work across market research, creative tests, retention, economy, paid acquisition, live events and regional launches. These disciplines often use separate dashboards and assumptions; faster AI-generated reports do not automatically make decisions more consistent.

Joyce is developing an AI-assisted operating copilot that connects evidence-gathering to concrete go/no-go decisions. It is organised around repeatable stages: identifying the customer and problem, reviewing comparable products, testing core-loop and economy hypotheses, deciding when to add or stop investment, and updating the decision as new cohort evidence arrives. The innovation is the **decision framework and human-accountability architecture**, rather than a claim that an AI model alone can run a game business.

## Proposed nomination answer 2 — Joyce's personal contribution
Joyce designed the operating questions, review gates, risk boundaries and delivery specifications by translating her hands-on experience in data-driven games into reusable workflows. Her scope connects product direction, retention and LTV, economy and monetisation, LiveOps and global launch-readiness. At GGC, these have been formalised into public-facing capability and consulting modules, alongside prototype and operating work on games and consumer-AI products.

Human operators are required to decide whether to proceed, change scope or stop. AI can compare evidence, prepare alternatives and accelerate QA; it does not approve client claims, execute commitments, or silently substitute unverifiable estimates for measured results. This design prioritises commercial accountability, player experience and cross-cultural context over generation speed.

## Proposed nomination answer 3 — Results, evidence and limitations
The output to date is an operating framework expressed as scoped capabilities and practical workflows, supplemented by public industry writing, teaching and speaking engagements. The public GGC capability atlas describes 14 domains and 16 scoped services—these are **first-party platform inventory**, not evidence of 14 independent clients or proven industry adoption.

Joyce's book and independently listed speaking appearances provide evidence that the concepts have been shared beyond the company. However, title-specific AI-assisted operating gains, customer outcomes, external utilisation counts and financial impact require independently reviewable, authorised case evidence before numerical claims are inserted into a formal nomination. The strongest final submission will add at least one cleared before/after case with the exact baseline, period, owner, measured outcome and verification status.

## Proposed nomination answer 4 — Leadership and inspiration
Joyce pairs technological experimentation with a practical leadership proposition: senior judgment should become more accessible, while authority stays with accountable people. She works across product, data, operating and entrepreneurial communities and shares game-operations methods through a published book and public speaking. She also serves as a mentor within Tencent's entrepreneurship community.

The contribution she wants to make is to help emerging leaders evaluate AI on real operating evidence: what improved for users, how the result was measured, which assumptions failed and who took responsibility for the final decision. Additional documented outcomes of mentoring women or technology-career programmes should be included only when backed by named testimony or an authorised programme record.
