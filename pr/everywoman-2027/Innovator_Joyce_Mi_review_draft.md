**Innovation, context and challenge.** Across global entertainment technology, new data and AI tools have multiplied faster than a team’s ability to make reliable decisions from them. The problem is rarely the absence of a dashboard. It is that customer behaviour, cohort retention, revenue and social virality, game economy, experimentation, marketing, regional fit, LiveOps and organizational accountability are managed as disconnected functions. As a result, teams struggle to identify which signals matter, when an initiative should be revised and who owns a go/no-go decision.

**Joyce’s unconventional approach.** Joyce Mi has spent more than fifteen years designing operating systems for interactive products—bridging quantitative models, player experience and executive decisions rather than treating data as back-office reporting. As a Product Manager at Zynga, she led and coordinated multiple producers and owned product and commercial operating responsibilities for the Tencent edition of *CityVille*, the Chinese edition of *FarmVille*, and global games including *Bubble*, *Ruby* and *Slingo*. Her work connected DAU and revenue modelling, K-factor and social growth, cohort analysis, research, experimentation and monetization into practical game operations. She subsequently adapted and helped transfer these operating methods to Asian teams. This was not the invention of a single algorithm: the innovation was to make technology-supported product judgment repeatable across markets, disciplines and organizations.

At Gameloft, where Joyce was Head of Asian Operations, she led operating responsibilities for the global *Order & Chaos* and *Heroes of Order & Chaos* franchises and for *Minion Rush* in China. She built and adapted regional analytics, release, monetization and LiveOps coordination across more than ten studios and a network of 500-plus cross-functional collaborators. The scale cited is a collaborative network, not 500 direct reports. The underlying design challenge was the same as in an early game test: a shared decision architecture must translate raw data into aligned work by product, engineering, design, publishing and business teams.
