## A. Personal LinkedIn — operator insights (editorial flagship)

**Working headline: Great games need an operating system, not only great assets.**

I've spent years working with game teams across Zynga, Gameloft and development/publishing businesses. One pattern keeps recurring: the teams may all be doing their jobs well, but they don't always share the same definition of a launch-ready product.

A developer sees a stable build. Marketing sees a campaign. A regional publisher sees a new market. Analytics sees event counts. The player sees one experience—and the business must somehow reconcile all five.

The hardest handoff often happens **after** a distribution or publishing deal has been signed. Who owns the first-play experience? Which player metrics are reliable enough to approve paid spend? Who can change the economy or LiveOps calendar? Who can say **stop** when the evidence doesn't justify the next market budget?

I put together a public operating charter for the **first 90 days of a game publishing partnership**: a responsibility map, a three-receipt evidence standard, a 30/60/90-day review cadence and an explicit scale / revise / hold / stop decision record.

This isn't a claim of guaranteed ROI. It's a way for both sides to ask sharper questions before a soft launch becomes a sunk-cost commitment.

## B. GGC Games company LinkedIn — useful operating resource

**A free 90-day game publishing operating charter**

A signing ceremony is not an operating plan.

GGC has published a practical framework for developer–publisher teams entering a new market. It covers:

• 7 workstreams with named decision owners — product, first play, rights, data, game economy, P&L and escalation  
• 3 independent evidence receipts — build, first-play and measured audience/economics  
• Days 1–14 / 15–30 / 31–60 / 61–90 operating reviews  
• A reusable decision record for limited test, improve, scale, hold or stop

The framework is designed as a discussion tool; it is not legal advice, a set of universal KPI targets or a claim that GGC manages the reader's business.

## G. Newsletter opening (author-owned)

**Subject:** The first 90 days after a global game publishing agreement

When a developer signs with a publisher, the parties have a contract. That contract does not guarantee that they share the same operating plan. Launch readiness, player evidence, regional responsibilities, marketing spend and LiveOps can become disconnected immediately after the deal closes.

Our latest practitioner framework maps the developer–publisher handoff into seven workstreams, three independent receipts and a 90-day operating calendar. It asks which evidence justifies the next market budget, what is still assumed, and who has the authority to revise or stop the plan.

This is not a case study claiming a specific lift, and it does not prescribe universal retention targets. It is a reusable decision tool for founders, product leads and publishers who need to move from "ready to launch" to "ready to learn".
