## 00 — From Global Hits to Operating Systems (PRIMARY AUTHORITY TALK)

**Subtitle:** Building, transferring, and rebuilding the operating architecture behind global games across products, markets and organizations.

### Thesis

Repeatable global product results require more than isolated dashboards or creative campaigns. Joyce's career spans Zynga CityVille/FarmVille/Arcade operating practices; the first-party account of adapting DAU, revenue, social virality, research and experiment methods into Asian team contexts; Gameloft regional architectures connecting 10+ studios and a 500+ cross-functional network; and later entrepreneurial and cross-company rebuilds. The signature contribution is connecting player evidence, economy, LiveOps, release, commercialization, publishing, and organizational responsibility into one executable decision system. Recent AI-native work extends, rather than invents, that foundation.

### Audience takeaways

- What a full game operating architecture looks like from category and product through players, data, monetization, launch and portfolio.
- How methods are transferred across teams and markets without assuming one-size-fits-all.
- How to distinguish successful flagship outcomes from the replicable system behind them.
- Where organizational design, cross-functional ownership and measurable player outcomes meet.

### Proof and safeguards

Use Zynga's scoped Chinese CityVille D1 +700bps and the Gameloft 10+ studios/500+ cross-functional network with exact qualifiers. Industry-method adoption, ranking/No.1 claims and consulting/reader success numbers require project-by-project source and clearance. The 2024 book and verified 2019–2026 organizer programmes support public knowledge transfer, not quantified downstream adoption by themselves.

### Formats

- Preferred: 25–40 minute independent featured/keynote-style presentation or fireside with senior host.
- Secondary: executive closed-door session; panel only if peers/role/recording justify it.

## 01 — AI Is Rebuilding the Game Business

**Subtitle:** Beyond asset production: product decisions, LiveOps, monetization, publishing and operating systems.

### Thesis

The first wave of game AI focused on faster content production. The larger shift is operational: AI is beginning to change how teams research markets, evaluate products, design experiments, run LiveOps, reason about monetization, prepare creative tests and support executive decisions. The advantage is not “more prompts”; it is a better decision system with explicit evidence, evaluation and human approval.

### Audience takeaways

- A practical map of where AI belongs across the game operating lifecycle.
- How to distinguish useful automation from low-quality output volume.
- Where human review, evidence state and decision ownership must remain explicit.
- A staged path from AI-assisted tasks to an AI-native operating system.

### Formats

- 15 min: five operating shifts + one implementation map.
- 30 min: full operating lifecycle + examples + governance.
- 45 min: full talk + category examples + implementation checklist.

## 02 — How Global Games Actually Win

**Subtitle:** Product → Soft Launch → LiveOps → Monetization → Global Publishing.

### Thesis

Global success is not a sequence of disconnected functional optimizations. Product loop, FTUE, retention, monetization, LiveOps, UA economics, localization and publishing form one operating system. Teams lose time when each function reports its own “good” metrics while the player and P&L tell a different story.

### Audience takeaways

- What to validate before and during soft launch.
- How early-session behavior connects to D1/D7, payer conversion and LTV.
- Why content cadence and event burden must be designed against team capacity.
- How to build a cross-functional launch review rather than a dashboard collection.
- What changes when moving from domestic product success to global publishing.

### Formats

- 15 min: the five launch gates.
- 30 min: operating blueprint + examples.
- 45 min: blueprint + soft-launch review template + executive decision gates.

## 03 — What Should We Build Next?

**Subtitle:** A decision framework for Casino, Arcade, Sims and AI-native consumer products.

### Thesis

The difficult question is rarely “can we build this?” It is whether a category, loop, content burden and commercial model fit the team, market and capital. Category choice should be treated as an explicit thesis with kill criteria, not as trend-following.

### Audience takeaways

- How to separate category attractiveness from team fit.
- The “3 seconds / 30 minutes / Day 7” lens for early product design.
- How art burden, content burden, event burden and UA economics interact.
- What evidence is enough to proceed, pivot or stop.
- How AI-native consumer interaction changes product discovery and monetization tests.

### Formats

- 15 min: category decision matrix.
- 30 min: matrix + product loop examples.
- 45 min: matrix + category cases + greenlight / kill-criteria workshop.
