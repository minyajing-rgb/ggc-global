- **Proposed speaker type:** Independent game-industry practitioner (no paid/sponsor content).
- **Suggested title:** *China–Latin America Game Publishing: What to Validate Before a Deal Becomes a Business*
- **Alternative title:** *Beyond the Publishing Contract: The First 90 Days of a Cross-Market Launch*

**Abstract (proposed original, for editorial application):**

International publishing partnerships often look straightforward on paper: a studio delivers a game, a publisher handles a region, and the commercial upside is shared. In practice, the two organizations may not agree on what "ready" means, what player evidence is credible, which team can approve media spend, or who owns the LiveOps decisions after launch. Drawing on operator experience across global and Asian game businesses, this session maps the first 90 days of a cross-market publishing partnership into five gates: public-build and first-play verification; content, rights and localization review; comparable acquisition and retention cohorts; market-specific economy and payback assumptions; and a clear, human-owned scale/iterate/stop decision. The framework is designed for studios and publishers assessing new regions, including teams exploring partnerships between Latin America, China and the rest of Asia. It is not a report of proprietary Brazilian market statistics or a guarantee of market-entry success. Participants leave with a practical partner-and-operator handoff checklist.

**Audience:** Studio founders, publishers, business development leads, game producers, product managers.  
**Three takeaways:** 
1. A five-gate cross-market publishing readiness checklist.
2. A 30/60/90-day division of responsibilities between publisher and development team.
3. A decision note that separates observed player outcomes from forecasts.

## Priority B — Develop:Brighton 2027: business lecture

- **Event:** 13–15 July 2027, Brighton, United Kingdom.
- **Official CFP:** https://www.developconference.com/get-involved/call-for-speakers-sarah/

- **Track:** Business; alternatives Mobile or Indie.
- **Proposed title:** *Beyond the Deal: The First 90 Days of a Global Publishing Partnership*
- **Speaker bio:** Use approved scope below, not unverified company revenue.
- **Official preparation:** The organizer instructs applicants to draft in its Word template first and preserve the completed copy; it says there is no post-submit editing. Link: https://www.developconference.com/images/uploads/docs/Speaker_Subs_Form_Word_Doc_2027.docx

**Lecture abstract:**

A signed publishing agreement does not automatically create an operating business. When development, publishing, LiveOps, growth and regional teams use different definitions of readiness, even experienced studios can invest in the wrong next step. This lecture offers an operator's framework for the first 90 days of international publishing, from the public build and first-play proof through localization, rights checks, cohort measurement and monetization economics. The central method is to distinguish three independent receipts—build evidence, unassisted player experience and comparable audience evidence—then translate them into a 30/60/90-day responsibilities chart. The audience learns which facts justify a limited market test, when to revisit a contract assumption, who should approve incremental acquisition spending, and how to record a reversible scale/stop decision. The approach draws on product-commercial roles at Zynga, regional cross-studio operations at Gameloft and founder-led game business experience, using public or anonymized cases. No unpublished employer KPI, client contract or unverified uplift will be shown.

**Session outline (45 min):**
- 0–6 min — The development-to-publishing handoff problem;
- 6–15 min — Build, first-play and audience receipts;
- 15–27 min — 30/60/90 responsibilities and KPI/data dictionary;
- 27–37 min — LiveOps, payback, and scale/iterate/stop case framework;
- 37–45 min — Practical checklist + attendee Q&A.

**Participant takeaways:** one responsibilities matrix, one five-gate checklist, one decision record template.

## Priority D — IEEE CoG 2027: industry presentation / method demonstration

- **Event:** 2–5 August 2027, Aizuwakamatsu, Japan.
- **Official page:** https://cog2027.u-aizu.ac.jp/
- **Portal stated opening:** 27 November 2026.
- **Industry-participation deadline:** 28 May 2027 (separate from papers / tutorial proposal dates).
- **Industry Day proposal requires only title + abstract** per organizer site. Sponsorship is a separate option, *not* a prerequisite for ordinary industry talk (reconfirm in portal terms).

**Title:** *Evidence-Gated AI Game Operations: Verifying the Build, the Player and the Commercial Decision*.

**Abstract:**

As game studios integrate generative AI into prototyping, localization, creative production and LiveOps reporting, the speed of artifacts can exceed the speed of validation. This industry presentation describes a traceable three-receipt protocol for separating engineering build completion, a player's unassisted first-session experience and defined audience outcomes. It proposes a five-part decision record—decision, observed proof, unknowns, test limits and accountable owner—so that cross-functional teams can audit what an AI-assisted recommendation actually supports. Illustrative games-industry scenarios show how repeated analytics events differ from deduplicated players and why total monthly revenue divided by current downloads is not cohort lifetime value. The contribution is an operating and measurement method, not a claim of experimentally validated AI ROI. It is intended to stimulate exchange between academic player-experience measurement researchers and practitioners making launch, quality and commercial decisions.
