# China ↔ Global Games Operating Brief — practitioner method card v0

**Editorial state:** GGC-authored working draft, 4 November 2026. For review under [Issue #23](https://github.com/minyajing-rgb/ggc-global/issues/23) and selective reuse in the existing [English report, Issue #9](https://github.com/minyajing-rgb/ggc-global/issues/9). No institution has commissioned, endorsed or published it. The five gates are GGC's proposed operating method, not a government standard, market benchmark or legal opinion.

## Page 1 — five decisions before cross-market scale

**Audience:** a game team considering a specific new market after a promising demo, creative test or limited launch. First name the market, store, product version and accountable decision owner. Use the *same cohort and time window* when comparing product signals. Do not turn one good ad result into a full launch decision.

| Gate | Decision to make | Evidence to put on the table | Advance only when… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Proposition and creative** | Is there a defined audience that understands the proposition? | Test dates, geography, placement, audience definition, creative variants, spend/sample, attribution settings, install or qualified-visit path and uncertainty. | The signal survives a repeat test or a pre-agreed validation check; a high click rate alone does not establish product demand. |
| **2. First-session product** | Can target users understand and return to the core loop? | Versioned onboarding funnel, first-session completion, repeat-play cohorts, qualitative confusion points and sample limitations. Segment by market and acquisition source. | The team can explain both the drop-offs and the next product change; a polished demo or aggregate retention figure is insufficient. |
| **3. Economy and LiveOps** | Can rewards, content and spending form a sustainable, fair loop? | Currency sources/sinks, progression bottlenecks, purchase and non-purchase paths, event cadence, payer/non-payer cohorts, support complaints and platform policy checks. | A named owner has tested the economy hypothesis without relying on an unverified revenue or LTV forecast. |
| **4. Regional readiness** | Can the team operate the product responsibly in this market? | Local-language comprehension, cultural/content review, store and advertising rules, privacy/consent, age controls, payments/refunds, customer support and applicable legal review. | Each required approval has an owner and evidence; translating the interface does not satisfy this gate. |
| **5. Bounded scale or stop** | What is the smallest next investment justified by gates 1–4? | Pre-set decision thresholds, test budget and duration, attribution limits, owner, review date, rollback/stop condition and unresolved risks. | The decision record says *advance, revise, hold or stop* and why. A model recommendation does not transfer accountability from the operator. |

**Use the gates as questions, not universal numeric thresholds.** Targets depend on product, genre, market, cohort, acquisition route and commercial rights. Any published case statistic needs the original source, definition, period and permission. Social casino/free-to-play examples must be separated from regulated real-money gambling.

**20-minute workshop exercise (proposal, not booked):** 3 minutes to define the market and decision; 7 minutes to locate the weakest of the five evidence gates; 5 minutes to fill the worksheet using the team's own permitted data; 5 minutes to choose one bounded test and a stop condition. The facilitator need not receive confidential dashboards. An anonymized example may be used only after the case owner approves both facts and publication rights.

**English reuse for Issue #9 — proposed executive frame:** “Cross-market launch is a sequence of evidence decisions. Creative response, repeat play, product economy, regional operating readiness and bounded scale each answer a different question. A team should record the cohort, source, decision owner and stop condition at every gate. This is a practitioner framework; it is not a substitute for local legal advice or independently sourced market statistics.”
