## Three priority solo talks (non-promotional)

### 01. China-Built, Globally Operated: Connecting Development Quality to Publishing ROI
**Best for:** PG Connects Shanghai; PG Connects Barcelona; industry / business strategy tracks. **Format:** 20–30 min solo, can adapt to 40 min.

**Abstract:** A polished game, sophisticated art and a working build do not automatically make a viable international publishing business. Drawing on real operating experience spanning Chinese and global game teams, this session explains how to convert development readiness into decisions that reflect regional player expectations and commercial reality. It links a reproducible first-play experience, localization ownership, cohort definitions, LiveOps cost, monetization and acquisition payback to one accountable decision: launch, improve, scale or stop. Attendees receive a practical evidence-and-decision checklist that both developers and publishers can use before committing the next market budget.

**Takeaways:** Distinguish build, player and business readiness; define common denominators and data owners; make go/no-go decisions explicit.

### 02. Entering China Without Guesswork: What Global Studios Must Get Right Before Signing a Publisher
**Best for:** European/US developer communities, PG Connects Barcelona/London, trade-association meetings. **Format:** Solo or operator fireside.

**Abstract:** International studios are often told that entering China is a matter of localization and distribution. The difficult choices begin earlier: whom to partner with, which responsibilities and rights to negotiate, what evidence demonstrates genuine product fit, who pays for market adaptation, and how studio and publisher share the operating decisions after launch. This session offers a practical, non-legal framework based on cross-market game operations and public-safe examples. Attendees leave with a partner-evaluation and first-90-day responsibility checklist rather than generic market-size projections.

**Takeaways:** Partner fit and contract-operating boundaries; localization and product test ownership; decision rights and post-launch cadence.

### 03. Beyond the Deal: The First 90 Days of a Global Publishing Partnership
**Best for:** Publisher Power, developer–publisher roundtables, Naavik / industry-business interviews. **Format:** 20–30 min solo or one-on-one interview.

**Abstract:** A publishing agreement creates expectations but does not automatically create an operating team. During the first 90 days, developers and publishers must align the build-to-public-player journey, event definitions, market responsibilities, launch budgets, retention/economy review and escalation authority. Without these handoffs, each party can report progress while the combined business lacks an accountable owner. This talk gives an evidence-led decision system for the launch handoff and highlights the signals that should trigger a revised plan, not another meeting.

**Takeaways:** Shared 30/60/90-day plan; one owner per decision; commercial evidence, unknowns and stop conditions.

## The intellectual property: Joyce OS and GGC Principle

**Joyce OS — seven connected domains:** Insight → Judgment → Validation → Globalization → Operations → Organization → Compounding.  
**GGC Principle:** Twelve operating principles, including validate before scaling, cash flow as a commercial test, fit systems to organizations, keep decision ownership human, and use AI to amplify capability rather than impersonate proof. These are **Joyce/GGC-origin frameworks**, not pre-existing accredited industry standards.

**Best presentation:** One practical visual of an operating decision → anonymized game example → the proof required to proceed → reusable checklist, with a short book reference at the end. Avoid turning a conference session into a consultancy sales pitch.
