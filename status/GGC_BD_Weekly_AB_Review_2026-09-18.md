## 4. Winning / losing message patterns

### Strongest observed engagement pattern
1. **Specific product/company trigger in subject** — e.g. product sequel, current portfolio shift, a measurable current operating transition.
2. **A decision question rather than a capability list** — “what compounds next?”, “which operating layer is missing?”, “product/market/commercialization — where is the current decision?”
3. **Direct/named route** where available.
4. **Short proof of relevance** from Joyce, then CTA.

### Losing / lower-quality pattern
- Reusing a broad paragraph listing global expansion + self-publishing + monetization + LiveOps + organization across many historical accounts.
- Identical generic fund subject such as “China game/AI dealflow + operating DD / portfolio support” across a large batch.
- Pitching mature publishers/studios as if they lack a full operating team; this directly triggered the “fully staffed internal team” objection.
- General/support/info inboxes when a named decision-maker, business-development route or official submission form exists.
- Sending volume before route verification. The Sep 18 ChatGPT run’s **9% hard-bounce rate** is the clearest immediate quality warning.

---

## 5. Repeated objections / failure reasons

1. **“We already have an internal team.”** → ICP/positioning issue. Reframe toward a project-specific external operating layer, benchmark, DD, launch, market entry or temporary missing corner; otherwise deprioritize.
2. **“No immediate need / later.”** → urgency issue. Nurture; follow only when there is a new product, market, funding, hiring or performance trigger.
3. **Wrong route / auto-routing.** → contact-quality issue. Several general/contact/sales/pitch inboxes produced automated responses instead of reaching a decision-maker.
4. **Invalid/stale mailbox/domain.** → sourcing hygiene issue. Suppress known failures and require re-verification before any repeat attempt.

## 6. Best-performing target profile — directional, not yet statistically proven

The highest-quality pipeline profile remains:

**Chinese-speaking / China-based founder-led or GM-led game team with proven R&D/commercial traction and an explicit operating transition** — domestic → global, publisher-dependent → self-publishing, co-dev → own IP, strong UA → weak monetization/LTV, or multi-studio growth without a unified operating system.

Secondary strong profiles:
- Publisher / platform with an explicit external-developer or partner route.
- AI strategic partner where GGC adds the game operating/GTM layer, not generic AI consulting.
- Active product transition (sequel, new market, portfolio reset, current hiring/build-out) with a named decision-maker.

Deprioritize: mature fully staffed companies without a specific trigger, low-scale studios unlikely to support annual-retainer economics, and generic investor lists without a clear portfolio-operating mandate.
