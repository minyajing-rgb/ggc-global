# Joyce Proprietary Playbook

> Version: 2026-10-08  
> Working title: **Joyce Operator's Rules / 独家判断秘籍**  
> Public use: show the existence and shape of the system.  
> Paid/internal use: scoring logic, thresholds, templates, case details, implementation sequence.

# 1. Category Judgment — 不追热闹，追结构

## Rule 1 — Look for structural value, not trend heat
A category is attractive when several things can coexist:
- real player demand
- repeatable content or system depth
- viable acquisition
- monetization headroom
- long-term operating space

## Rule 2 — Compare business models, not only visual competitors
The most useful competitor may look different but share:
- player motivation
- monetization
- session pattern
- progression
- content burden
- acquisition model

## Rule 3 — Ask whether the category fits the team
A theoretically good market is still a bad bet for the wrong organization.

---

# 2. Project Selection — 立项不是写BP，是下注

## Rule 4 — Define the product promise in one sentence
If the team cannot describe the player promise clearly, adding features will not solve the problem.

## Rule 5 — Separate "interesting" from "investable"
An idea becomes investable only when:
- the player promise is legible
- the test is possible
- the team can execute
- the business model has a path

## Rule 6 — Decide what must be true
Before building, write the 3–5 assumptions that must hold.

If those assumptions cannot be tested, the project is not ready.

---

# 3. Validation — Demo不是产品，数字不是答案

## Rule 7 — Test one primary question per stage

### Idea
Does anyone care?

### Demo
Can users understand and enjoy the core action?

### Market
Can the promise attract attention at a reasonable cost?

### Retention
Is there a reason to come back?

### Revenue
Does value creation support monetization?

## Rule 8 — Evidence quality matters more than evidence quantity
100 weak signals do not equal one strong signal.

## Rule 9 — Do not use traffic to hide product weakness
A strong campaign can create installs.  
It cannot create durable product value by itself.

---

# 4. Growth / Distribution — 买量首先是研究

## Rule 10 — Treat creative as product research
Creative tests:
- fantasy
- need state
- audience
- positioning
- emotional hook
- visual language

## Rule 11 — Separate CPI from acquisition quality
Cheap users are not automatically good users.

Look at:
- post-install behavior
- return
- payer quality
- cohort value
- market fit

## Rule 12 — Soft launch is a decision system
A soft launch is not "publish quietly".

It should answer:
- what works
- what fails
- what is fixable
- what is structural
- whether to scale

---

# 5. Retention / LiveOps — 留存不是数字，是回来理由

## Rule 13 — Always name the return reason
Examples:
- progress
- collection
- competition
- relation
- ownership
- expression
- curiosity
- reward
- status

If the team cannot name the return reason, retention work is likely becoming feature work.

## Rule 14 — LiveOps must change the player's tomorrow
A real LiveOps system creates:
- anticipation
- fresh goals
- changing social context
- new economic choices
- new identity / collection value

## Rule 15 — Content cadence is an economic decision
Every content-heavy product has a production burden.

Judge:
- asset cost
- player consumption speed
- reuse
- modularity
- lifecycle

---

# 6. Monetization — 付费不是弹窗，是价值交换

## Rule 16 — Payer behavior starts in product design
Good monetization usually depends on:
- perceived value
- urgency
- identity
- progression
- status
- convenience
- collection
- social context

## Rule 17 — ARPU without payer structure can mislead
Separate:
- payer conversion
- payer frequency
- payer depth
- whale dependency
- cohort quality

## Rule 18 — Payback is a strategy constraint
A business can have good LTV and still be impossible to scale if:
- cash cycle is too long
- acquisition is volatile
- content cost is too high
- operational overhead is too heavy

---

# 7. Organization — 大公司和创业公司不能用同一套动作

## Rule 19 — Match decision system to company size
Large companies often need:
- fewer interfaces
- clearer ownership
- faster escalation
- portfolio discipline

Startups often need:
- fewer priorities
- shorter validation loops
- founder clarity
- capital discipline

## Rule 20 — Protect knowledge continuity
If a product collapses when one person leaves, the team has hidden operating debt.

Preserve:
- decisions
- models
- data definitions
- operating history
- key relationships
- release logic

## Rule 21 — In turnaround, salvage assets before rebuilding
First identify:
- users
- revenue
- product value
- data
- code
- content
- team knowledge
- channel relationships

Then decide what deserves rebuilding.

---

# 8. Globalization — 出海不是把国内版本搬出去

## Rule 22 — Start with market truth
Different markets may differ in:
- fantasy
- price tolerance
- social norms
- platform behavior
- channel economics
- regulation

## Rule 23 — Adapt the product before adapting the copy
Localization cannot repair a fundamentally wrong product-market assumption.

## Rule 24 — Global publishing is an operating capability
It is the combination of:
- product
- positioning
- creative
- UA
- channel
- platform
- data
- LiveOps
- monetization

---

# 9. AI-Native — AI增加产量，不自动增加正确率

## Rule 25 — Automate repeatability, not accountability
Automate:
- collection
- formatting
- variant generation
- monitoring
- routine analysis

Keep human accountability for:
- category choice
- product judgment
- risk
- capital
- ethics
- final decision

## Rule 26 — Turn expert behavior into reusable infrastructure
The progression is:

```text
Experience → Method → Template → Tool → Workflow → Agent → Product
```

Not every step needs to become software.

## Rule 27 — AI-native means redesigning the workflow
It does not mean inserting a chatbot into every process.

---

# 10. The Joyce Review Questions

Use these ten questions in any major product review:

1. **What problem / desire are we solving for the player?**
2. **Why this category and why now?**
3. **What must be true for the product to win?**
4. **Which assumption has the weakest evidence?**
5. **What is the cheapest credible next test?**
6. **What is the real return reason?**
7. **What is the monetization mechanism, not just the price?**
8. **What is the biggest operating burden?**
9. **Does the organization match the product stage?**
10. **If this works, what becomes reusable for the next project?**

# 11. When People Should Call Joyce

The strongest-fit situations:

- entering a new category
- preparing global publishing
- deciding whether to continue investing
- reviewing a black-horse competitor
- fixing a mature product's second growth curve
- rebuilding a stalled / broken project
- connecting product, creative, UA, LiveOps, and monetization
- designing AI-native operating workflows
- needing founder / executive-level judgment without another generic vendor layer
