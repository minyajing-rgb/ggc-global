# GGC Positioning → Sourcing → Close

## 0. Core Rule

Do not start with "who can I email?"

Start with:

**What exactly is valuable → compared with what → to whom → in which buying situation → why now → what proof reduces risk → who decides → how they buy → what must happen for value to be delivered.**

Positioning comes before storytelling.
Qualification comes before scaling outreach.
Delivery design comes before promising outcomes.

---

# 1. Offer / Project Business Case Canvas

For every offer or project, complete this before sourcing.

## A. What is it?
- category
- one-line description
- maturity/stage
- commercial model
- price / financing / contract shape
- current geography

## B. Competitive alternatives
What would the buyer do if this did not exist?
- do nothing / status quo
- internal team
- another vendor/project
- another investment category
- postpone the decision
- use a cheaper substitute

## C. Differentiated capabilities / attributes
Only list differences that can be evidenced.

## D. Buyer value
Translate features into:
- revenue
- margin
- growth
- risk reduction
- time saved
- capital efficiency
- strategic option value
- reputation / defensibility

## E. Who cares most?
Define the characteristics of buyers who care disproportionately about this value.

## F. Why now?
Find the current trigger:
- new budget / fund cycle
- new product / market
- hiring / org change
- regulation
- funding
- performance inflection
- competitive pressure
- capacity bottleneck
- customer order
- proven acquisition signal
- launch deadline

## G. Proof
Rank proof from strongest to weakest:
1. audited / contractual / first-party operating data
2. verified product/market data
3. customer / partner evidence
4. credible third-party evidence
5. founder/user statement
6. hypothesis

Never mix these levels.

## H. Risks / objections
State the real reasons a buyer may say no:
- weak evidence
- wrong timing
- wrong ticket
- unclear ownership
- regulatory risk
- implementation burden
- unclear ROI
- team risk
- alternatives are good enough

Packaging must reduce risk, not hide it.

---

# 2. Positioning

Use this sequence:

**Alternatives → Differentiated Attributes → Value → Best-Fit Customer → Market Category**

Output:
- one-line positioning
- 3 differentiated values
- 3 buyer types that care most
- anti-ICP
- category / context in which the offer makes the most sense

Do not build a clever story before these inputs are clear.

---

# 3. Buyer / Investor Persona

A useful persona is not demographics.

For each persona define:
- what they are responsible for
- what success looks like
- what they fear
- what they already use/do instead
- what evidence they trust
- what triggers them into market
- what budget/check size they control
- who can veto
- who can champion
- how they decide
- how they buy/sign

The question is not:
"Who could buy?"

It is:
**"Who would care more than average, now, and has a credible path to action?"**

---

# 4. Category Entry Points / Buying Situations

Map the situations in which buyers start looking.

Use:
- Why?
- When?
- While doing what?
- Where?
- With/for whom?
- With what?
- How feeling / under what pressure?

Prioritize buying situations by:
1. commonness
2. credibility — can we credibly solve it?
3. competitiveness — can we stand out?

Marketing and sales content should repeatedly link the brand/offer to the most valuable buying situations.

---

# 5. Brand System: Discoverable → Credible → Winnable

## Discoverable
Can the right buyers find and remember us?
Assets:
- expert content
- category research
- search/social presence
- events/communities
- referral networks
- recognizable point of view

## Credible
Can buyers believe the claims?
Assets:
- operating proof
- customer/project cases
- expert credentials
- references
- transparent data
- visible methodology
- risk disclosure

## Winnable
Can the buyer defend choosing us internally?
Assets:
- clear business case
- ROI/economic logic
- implementation/delivery plan
- decision criteria
- risk controls
- comparison vs status quo
- stakeholder-specific proof

A strong brand is not "looks famous."
It is **easy to remember, easy to trust, and easy to justify choosing.**

---

# 6. Packaging Ladder

Every offer/project should have the same information at different depths.

1. **5-second label**
   What category is this?

2. **15-second hook**
   What is it + strongest proof + why now?

3. **60-second pitch**
   Problem/opportunity → differentiated value → proof → ask/CTA.

4. **One-page brief**
   Buyer-friendly facts, not a mini deck.

5. **Proof pack**
   data / screenshots / cases / contracts / methodology / references.

6. **Full deck / BP / proposal**
   for real evaluation.

7. **DD / procurement room**
   questions, risks, legal, financial, implementation, ownership.

Do not send level 6 when level 2 is enough to get the next step.

---

# 7. Sourcing

Build the target universe from the positioning.

## Account filter
- category fit
- size/ticket fit
- geography
- current timing signal
- ability to buy/invest
- known alternatives
- relationship path
- decision-maker availability

## Decision graph
Map:
- Economic Buyer
- Champion
- technical/product evaluator
- operating user
- legal/compliance/procurement
- blockers
- influencers

For investor deals map:
- thesis owner
- check-size owner
- sector Partner/Principal
- CVC/Corp Dev lead
- warm path
- official intake

## Signal-based priority
A smaller account with a strong current trigger can outrank a bigger account with no timing.

---

# 8. Message-Market Fit

Do not personalize with trivia.
Personalize around **business relevance**.

Every first message should answer:
1. Why you?
2. Why this?
3. Why now?
4. Why believe me/us?
5. What is the smallest useful next step?

Different stakeholders get different proof and value.

---

# 9. Qualification / Deal Control

Adapt MEDDPICC-style thinking to the deal:

- **Metrics** — what measurable outcome matters?
- **Economic Buyer** — who can authorize the money?
- **Decision Criteria** — what will they judge?
- **Decision Process** — how is the decision made?
- **Paper Process** — procurement/legal/signature/payment.
- **Pain / Opportunity** — why act?
- **Champion** — who wants this to happen?
- **Competition** — including status quo / no decision.

If critical fields remain unknown, the deal is not "late stage" merely because people are friendly.

---

# 10. Close

Closing is not pressure.
It is reducing ambiguity.

For every live opportunity maintain:
- agreed problem/opportunity
- agreed value
- stakeholder map
- decision criteria
- unresolved risk
- owner of each action
- next date
- commercial terms
- legal/paper process
- delivery start condition

Use a mutual action plan for material deals.

A meeting without a next action is not progress.

---

# 11. Delivery-to-Renewal

Sales promise and delivery scope must match.

Before signature define:
- outcome
- deliverables
- buyer responsibilities
- seller responsibilities
- data/access required
- milestones
- acceptance criteria
- escalation path
- reporting cadence
- commercial change-control

After delivery:
**Proof → Case → Referral → Expansion / Renewal**

This is how brand and pipeline compound.

---

# 12. Continuous Commercial Loop

The best sales/marketing system is:

**Positioning → ICP → Buying Situation → Content/Brand → Sourcing → Conversation → Qualification → Close → Delivery → Proof → Stronger Positioning**

Every win/loss should update:
- who cares most,
- which proof works,
- which objections matter,
- which signals predict intent,
- which stories are credible,
- which accounts should not be pursued.

---

# 13. Minimum Output Before Scaling

Before any large campaign, produce:
- Business Case Canvas
- Positioning
- ICP + anti-ICP
- 3–5 buying situations
- persona / decision graph
- packaging ladder
- proof inventory
- objections/risks
- sourcing criteria
- message variants
- qualification fields
- close/delivery handoff

If these are missing, more outreach usually produces more noise, not more revenue.
