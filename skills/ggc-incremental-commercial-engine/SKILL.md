# GGC Incremental Commercial Engine

## 0. Principle

Do not run:
**List → Blast to CAP → Review at the end.**

Run:
**CAP → Qualified Cohort → Decision Graph → Controlled Wave → Stage Conversion → Diagnose → Tune One Variable → Next Wave → Scale.**

Every stage is multiplicative. A weak early stage compounds downstream.

---

# 1. Four CAPs

## CAP 1 — Market / Research CAP
All plausible accounts in the defined market.

This is a census number, not a sales target.

## CAP 2 — Qualified Account CAP
Accounts that pass hard ICP/thesis/economic/timing gates.

This is the true addressable commercial pool.

## CAP 3 — Decision-Maker CAP
For each qualified account, map the people who can move the decision.

### B2B annual-client sales
Target graph:
**3–5 core people/account**
- Founder / CEO
- GM / BU / Studio Head
- Publishing / Commercial / Business
- Product / Ops / LiveOps / Growth
- optional procurement/legal/finance only when deal is active

### FA investor development
Target graph:
**2–4 people/institution**
- primary Partner / Principal / Investment Director
- fallback sector investor
- optional senior sponsor
- official submission route

Decision graph CAP is not sending CAP.

## CAP 4 — Active Wave CAP
How many qualified accounts/people can be contacted before the next mandatory review.

Default:
- First calibration wave: 30 qualified accounts
- Standard wave: 50–100 qualified accounts
- Scale wave: 100–250 only after prior wave passes quality gates

For B2B, first-touch concurrency:
- normally 1 primary + at most 1 secondary executive/account in the first wave
- retain the remaining 1–3 mapped executives as fallback/multi-thread paths
- expand only with distinct role-specific angles and clean account history

For FA:
- normally primary investor first
- then fallback / official route
- do not spray 3–4 investors in one fund simultaneously

# 5. Market Benchmarks — External Reference

Use external benchmarks as orientation, not as GGC truth.

### Cold email
Gong research:
- average ~344 cold emails per meeting
- reply → meeting: ~15.3% average; ~29.6% top reps
- top cold emails tend to be short (roughly 100 words or less / 3–4 sentences)
- C-level executives reply materially less often than non-executives

Cognism 2025 internal outbound:
- SDR cold email reply rate: ~8.98% with verified data and multi-channel execution
- cited broader B2B average near ~5%

### Generic B2B downstream
HubSpot public benchmark guidance:
- overall lead → customer often around 2–5%
- qualified lead → opportunity roughly 15–25%
- demo → proposal roughly 20–30%
- proposal → close roughly 25–40%

These populations are not identical to GGC high-value consulting; use them as sanity ranges only.

# 9. Incremental Wave SOP

## Wave 0 — Readiness
Before any send:
- positioning complete
- ICP/anti-ICP complete
- evidence/proof inventory
- decision graph fields
- suppression/history
- route quality
- pitch variants
- stage definitions

## Wave 1 — 30 Qualified Accounts
Objective: validate list + route + message.

B2B:
- map 90–150 executives
- contact 30–60 people, normally 1–2/account

FA:
- 30 institutions
- 30 primary investors + fallback mapping

Review after:
- 30 qualified accounts OR
- 50 delivered messages,
whichever occurs later.

## Wave 2 — 50–100 Qualified Accounts
Only scale if:
- route failure <3–5%
- human reply >=3–4%
- meaningful need/interest exists
- no STOP/duplicate control failure
- decision-maker coverage >=60% of target graph

Change only one major variable:
- ICP,
- trigger,
- persona,
- opening,
- proof,
- CTA,
- channel,
not all at once.

## Wave 3 — 100–250 Accounts
Scale winner(s), keep holdout/control.

## Saturation Review
Before touching >50% of Qualified CAP:
- calculate response decay
- duplicate/brand risk
- segment performance
- untouched high-fit accounts
- referral/warm-path opportunities
- inbound/content contribution

Do not "pitch to full CAP and review later".

# 10. Funnel Diagnosis

## Leak A — Research → Qualified low
Problem:
wrong market definition / weak ICP.

Optimize:
category, size, economics, thesis, stage, geography.

## Leak B — Qualified → Decision Graph low
Problem:
poor account research.

Optimize:
official team pages, founder graph, events, LinkedIn, portfolio networks, warm paths.

## Leak C — Decision Graph → Contact Ready low
Problem:
route quality.

Optimize:
verified routes, warm intro, WeChat/phone/social, official business channels.

## Leak D — Delivered → Reply low
Problem:
message-market fit, timing, subject/opening, sender trust.

Optimize:
why-you, why-now, brevity, role relevance, proof, channel mix.

## Leak E — Reply → Qualified Need / Interest low
Problem:
wrong CTA or curiosity without commercial fit.

Optimize:
response handling, qualification questions, narrower offer/deal angle.

## Leak F — Reply → Meeting low
Problem:
high-friction CTA / weak next-step handling.

External reference:
average reply→meeting ~15.3%; top ~29.6%.

Optimize:
specific low-friction CTA, concrete meeting purpose, 2 time options when appropriate.

## Leak G — Meeting → Opportunity low
Problem:
weak discovery / unqualified meetings.

Optimize:
economic buyer, need, metrics, decision criteria/process, timing.

## Leak H — Opportunity → Proposal low
Problem:
no consensus / scope unclear.

Optimize:
mutual action plan, problem definition, scope, value and buyer responsibility.

## Leak I — Proposal → Signed low
Problem:
trust / price / business case / procurement / competition.

Optimize:
ROI logic, proof, pricing architecture, risk-reversal, stakeholder coverage, paper process.

## Leak J — Signed → Cash/Delivery low
Problem:
bad commercial operations.

Optimize:
payment terms, start conditions, owner, kickoff, acceptance criteria.

# 11. Pitch Risk Controls

## Message risk
Never:
- exaggerate data
- invent customer/revenue/profit
- use generic praise as personalization
- send contradictory narratives to the same account
- hide known material risk

## Brand risk
Decision graph does not mean simultaneous mass contact.

B2B:
- map 3–5
- first wave contact 1–2
- expand only if justified

FA:
- primary → fallback → official route

## Frequency risk
For cold B2B planning:
- cap new-account first-wave contacts at 1–2 people
- no mechanical legacy +5/+10 chasing
- after a new-contact no-response, use a distinct role/signal only when account remains qualified

Company-level STOP overrides all.

## Channel risk
If a source produces repeated hard failures:
pause and repair before scaling.

# 12. Incremental Economics Model

For any cohort:

Signed Deals =
Qualified Accounts
× Reachable Rate
× Contact Rate
× Delivery Rate
× Human Reply Rate
× Need Qualification Rate
× Meeting Rate
× Hold Rate
× Opportunity Rate
× Proposal Rate
× Close Rate

Revenue =
Signed Deals × Average Contract Value

FA Funded Amount =
Contact-Ready Investor Opportunities
× Interest Rate
× BP Rate
× Meeting Rate
× DD Rate
× Terms Rate
× Fund Rate
× Average Funded Amount

Do not optimize only the highest-visibility stage.
The biggest multiplicative gain often comes from fixing the weakest early gate.

# 13. Review Cadence

## Per wave
Review every 30 qualified accounts / 50 delivered messages.

## Weekly
Review:
- segment
- account source
- persona
- decision role
- route
- pitch
- CTA
- channel
- stage conversion
- time-in-stage
- reason lost

## Monthly
Re-estimate:
- Market CAP
- Qualified CAP
- Decision-Maker CAP
- untouched high-fit pool
- saturation
- conversion model
- capacity / sender reputation
- pipeline/revenue forecast

## Quarterly
Rebuild ICP and positioning from:
- wins
- losses
- delivery outcomes
- customer/investor feedback
- category changes.

---

# 14. Scale Rule

Scale only what is **qualified, reachable, measurable and repeatable**.

The goal is not:
"reach the cap."

The goal is:
**increase expected revenue / funded amount per qualified account while preserving the remaining market.**
